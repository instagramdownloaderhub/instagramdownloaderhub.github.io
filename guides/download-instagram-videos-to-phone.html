How to Download Instagram Videos to Your Phone: iPhone and Android

Save Instagram videos on your phone the safe way, with the official options for iPhone and Android.

Saving an Instagram video to your phone sounds simple, but Instagram is deliberately restrictive about it, because videos belong to their creators and Instagram's business depends on people watching inside the app. That does not mean you are stuck: there are official options on both iPhone and Android, and knowing which one applies to each type of video saves a lot of frustration. This guide covers them all.

Save inside Instagram first

For any video, tap the bookmark icon to save it to your Saved collection. It stays in the app, it is private, and you can find it later from your profile. This works for feed videos, Reels, and IGTV-style long videos alike. The catch is that saved videos need an internet connection, so this is a bookmark, not an offline file. For most people, this is enough, because the point is usually to find the video again, not to own a copy of it.

Download a Reel on Android

On Android, open the Reel, tap Share, and look for the Download option. When the creator allows downloads, the video saves to your gallery with an Instagram watermark. The process is quick and needs no extra app. If the option is missing, the creator disabled downloads or the music licence blocks it. Android also lets you screen-record a video you are watching, but that is only appropriate for your own content or content you have permission to record.

Download a Reel on iPhone

On iPhone the same Share, then Download option appears when the creator allows it, and the video saves to the Photos app. iOS also has a built-in screen recorder in Control Centre, which is useful for your own videos and for recording a tutorial you made. Turn on Do Not Disturb before recording so notifications do not appear in the video. As on Android, a missing Download button means the creator or the licence has blocked it.

Avoid the downloader app trap

The App Store and Google Play are full of apps that claim to download any Instagram video. They usually work by asking for your login or by opening Instagram in a hidden browser, and they are frequently removed and replaced by copycats. The risks include stolen passwords, aggressive ads, and subscriptions that are hard to cancel. If an app needs your Instagram password to download a public video, it is not a legitimate tool. Stick to the official options and accept that some videos cannot be downloaded.

Free up space before downloading

A phone that is almost full will fail downloads, refuse to record video, and behave strangely in other ways, so free space is the first thing to check before you save anything. On iPhone, go to Settings, then General, then iPhone Storage, where you will see which apps use the most space. On Android, the same information is in Settings, then Storage. The usual culprits are old videos in your camera roll, downloaded podcasts you have already heard, and apps you have not opened in months. Back up photos to a cloud service and delete the local copies, remove watched downloads from the YouTube and Instagram apps, and clear the cache of apps that have grown large. Aim to keep at least five gigabytes free, because the phone needs working room as well as storage. A tidy phone downloads reliably; a full one does not.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I save Instagram videos to my camera roll?

Only when the creator allows downloads. Otherwise, use Instagram's Save feature, which keeps the video inside the app.

Is there an official Instagram download button?

For Reels where the creator allows it, yes, in the Share menu. For feed videos, Instagram offers Save, not a file download.

Are downloader apps safe?

Most are not. Many show aggressive ads, and any app asking for your Instagram password should be avoided.
