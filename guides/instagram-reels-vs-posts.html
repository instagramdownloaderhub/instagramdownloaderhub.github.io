Instagram Reels vs Feed Posts: Which Format to Save and Share

The differences between Reels and feed posts, and how saving, downloading, and sharing work for each.

Instagram has two main formats for sharing, and they behave differently when you try to save or share them. Reels are short vertical videos built for discovery, while feed posts are photos, carousels, and longer videos shown to your followers. Knowing the difference matters, because the download and save options are not the same for each format, and the right format for your content depends on what you are trying to achieve.

What a Reel is

A Reel is a vertical video, usually 15 to 90 seconds long, with music, effects, and text overlays. Reels appear in a dedicated feed, they are recommended to people who do not follow you, and they are the format Instagram promotes most. Creators can allow or block downloads of their Reels, and downloaded Reels carry a watermark with the creator's username. If you want reach, a Reel is usually the best choice, and if you want to save a Reel, the Share menu is the first place to look.

What a feed post is

A feed post is a photo, a set of up to 20 photos in a carousel, or a video that appears in the main feed of your followers. Feed posts support long captions, location tags, and product tags, and they stay on your profile grid permanently. They are better than Reels for detailed content, such as a recipe with steps, a product with several angles, or a photo series. Feed videos can be saved with the bookmark icon, but Instagram does not offer a file download for them.

Saving each format

For a Reel, tap the bookmark to save it, or use Download in the Share menu when the creator allows it. For a feed post, the bookmark is the option, and the post is stored in your Saved collection. Carousels save as a whole, and you can swipe through the images later. In both cases, saving is private, and the creator is not told. If you want to keep a collection, create named collections for each format, so your saved Reels and your saved posts do not mix into one unsearchable pile.

Sharing each format

Sharing a Reel sends a link that opens the Reel full-screen, which is ideal for a chat or a story. Sharing a feed post sends a link to the post on the creator's profile. Both can be shared to your own Story, where the original creator is credited. When you share someone else's content, always keep the credit intact and do not crop out a watermark. The formats are different tools for different jobs, and using the right one makes your content easier to find and easier to save.

Plan your content mix

A healthy Instagram account uses both Reels and feed posts, and the mix depends on your goal. If you want to reach new people, post Reels two or three times a week, because they are recommended to strangers. If you want to build trust with the followers you already have, post feed photos and carousels with detailed captions, because those are the posts your followers see and save. A practical weekly plan is two Reels, one carousel, and one single photo, with Stories every day to stay visible. Do not post the same content in both formats: a Reel should show movement, process, or personality, while a feed post should show detail, results, or information. Review your insights monthly to see which format your audience saves and shares, and shift the mix toward what works. Consistency matters more than volume, so choose a schedule you can keep.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between a Reel and a post?

A Reel is a short vertical video with music and effects. A feed post is a photo, a carousel, or a longer video shown in the main feed.

Can I download feed videos?

Instagram offers Save for feed videos, not a file download. Reels can be downloaded when the creator allows it.

Which format gets more reach?

Reels usually reach more new people, while feed posts are better for photos and detailed captions.
