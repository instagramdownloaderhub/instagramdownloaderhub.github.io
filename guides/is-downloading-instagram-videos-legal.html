Is Downloading Instagram Videos Legal? The Rules in Plain English

Copyright, Instagram's terms, and privacy rules for saving Instagram videos, explained simply.

The rules for downloading Instagram videos are similar to those for any social platform, but Instagram's community is more personal, and the privacy stakes are higher, because many accounts belong to private individuals rather than companies. This guide explains the three layers that matter: copyright law, Instagram's terms of service, and the privacy expectations of the people whose videos you want to save. It is general information, not legal advice.

Copyright applies to Instagram videos

Every video on Instagram is owned by the person who made it, unless they transferred the rights. Downloading a copy without permission is, in strict legal terms, making an unauthorised copy, and re-uploading it is a separate infringement. Exceptions for private copying, criticism, or research exist in some countries, but they are narrow and they do not cover building a collection of other people's videos. The fact that a video is public does not mean it is free to take: public visibility is not a licence.

Instagram's terms of service

Instagram's terms prohibit accessing content through unauthorised means, which includes most third-party downloader apps. Using those apps can lead to a warning, a temporary block, or a permanent account ban, and Instagram actively detects automated access. The official Save feature and the creator-approved Reel download are the sanctioned ways to keep a video. If you run a business account, a ban is a serious commercial risk, so the terms are worth respecting.

Privacy and consent

Many Instagram accounts are personal, and a video of a person at a family event or on holiday is not the same as a commercial video. Even when a video is public, the person in it may not want it downloaded, edited, or shared elsewhere. Before you save or share someone's video, ask whether they would be comfortable with it. If the video shows a child, be especially careful. When in doubt, share the link to the original post instead of keeping a copy, because the link respects the creator's control.

When downloading is clearly fine

Downloading your own videos is always fine, and Instagram's Archive and your camera roll make it easy. Videos released under an open licence are fine within the licence terms. Content you have explicit permission to use is fine, and a screenshot of a public post for a genuine news report or review can be lawful under fair use or fair dealing in some countries. The safest rule is simple: download only what is yours or openly licensed, use the official features, and link to everything else.

Report stolen content

If someone re-uploads your video without permission, Instagram gives you a way to have it removed. Open the offending post, tap the three dots, choose Report, and follow the copyright option, which asks for details about your original work. Instagram usually acts within a few days, and repeat offenders can lose their accounts. Keep evidence of your ownership: the original file on your phone, the date you posted it, and any earlier versions. A watermark with your username on your videos makes theft easier to prove and less attractive to thieves, because the stolen copy advertises the real creator. If the same account steals repeatedly, report each post separately rather than only one. You can also ask the account to remove it first, in a polite message, because some reposts are mistakes rather than malice, and a quick message often solves the problem faster than a formal report.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I download my own Instagram videos?

Yes. Videos you created are yours, and Instagram lets you save them from your archive or your camera roll.

What about videos with a Creative Commons licence?

Some creators license their work openly. Check the licence and give credit as it requires.

Can downloading get my account banned?

Using third-party downloader apps can break Instagram's terms and risk your account. The official features are safe.
