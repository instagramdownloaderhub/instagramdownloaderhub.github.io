Instagram Private vs Public Posts: What You Can Save and Share

How private and public Instagram accounts differ, and what that means for saving, sharing, and downloading.

Whether an Instagram account is public or private changes everything about what you can see, save, and share. Public posts are visible to anyone, while private posts are visible only to approved followers. This matters for downloading, because a tool cannot fetch what it cannot see, and it matters for privacy, because a private account is a deliberate choice by the owner. This guide explains the difference and what it means in practice.

What public means

A public account's posts, Reels, and profile picture are visible to anyone, including people without an Instagram account, in most cases. Public posts can appear in search, on the Explore page, and in recommendations. Anyone can save a public post with the bookmark icon, share its link, or, for Reels, download it when the creator allows. Public does not mean free to take, though: the creator still owns the content, and re-uploading it without permission is infringement.

What private means

A private account's posts are visible only to followers the owner has approved. A stranger who visits the profile sees only the profile picture, the bio, and the follower counts. Private posts do not appear in search or Explore, and downloader tools cannot fetch them, because the tool has no access to the account. If you follow a private account and it accepts you, you can save its posts inside Instagram, but you should not share them outside, because the owner chose a limited audience.

Switching between public and private

You can switch your account in Settings, then Privacy, then Account Privacy. Switching to private hides your existing posts from non-followers immediately, and switching back to public makes them visible again. Businesses and creators usually stay public, because they want to be discovered. Personal accounts, and accounts for children, are often better private. Think about it before you post something sensitive: once a public post has been screenshotted or shared, making the account private does not undo that.

Respecting the boundary

If someone's account is private, do not try to work around it with a fake account, a downloader, or a friend's access. That is a breach of trust and of Instagram's rules, and it can lead to your account being banned. If a private account posts something you need, such as a business's price list, ask the owner directly. The privacy setting is the owner telling you who they want to see their content, and the respectful response is to accept it.

Protect children's accounts

Children's Instagram accounts deserve extra care, and the safest choice is private. A private account means only approved followers can see posts, Stories, and Reels, which keeps a child's photos away from strangers. Turn off location tags on posts, because a tagged location can reveal a home, a school, or a regular routine. Talk to your child about who they accept as a follower: a follower should be someone they know in real life, not a stranger with a friendly profile picture. Review the follower list together every few months and remove anyone you do not recognise. Turn on two-factor authentication, use a strong password that only a parent knows, and check the privacy settings after every app update, because updates sometimes reset them. The goal is not to ban Instagram, but to make it a safe place for a child to share with people they trust.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I see a private account's posts?

Only if the owner approves your follow request. Otherwise their posts, Stories, and Reels are hidden from you.

Can I download a private post?

No. Private content is not accessible to downloader tools, and saving it without permission breaks Instagram's rules.

Should I make my account private?

If you want control over who sees your posts, yes. A private account hides your content from strangers and from search.
