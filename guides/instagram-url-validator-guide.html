Instagram URL Formats: Posts, Reels, Stories, and Profiles

Read any Instagram link: post URLs, Reel links, profile URLs, and how to tell them apart.

Instagram links look similar at first glance, but the part after the domain name tells you exactly what kind of content the link points to: a post, a Reel, a Story, or a profile. Knowing how to read a link helps you spot a broken link, tell a Reel from a photo post, and paste the right link into a tool. This guide explains each format and the common mistakes people make with Instagram links.

Post and Reel URLs

A feed post link looks like instagram.com/p/ABC123xyz/, where the code after /p/ is the post's shortcode. A Reel link looks like instagram.com/reel/ABC123xyz/, with the same kind of code after /reel/. The shortcode is unique to that post or Reel, and it is the part a tool needs to identify the content. Links copied from the Share button may be shortened or may include tracking parameters after a question mark, but the shortcode stays the same.

Profile and Story URLs

A profile link is simply instagram.com/username/, with nothing after the username. A Story link is temporary and looks like instagram.com/stories/username/123456789/, with a number identifying the Story, and it stops working after 24 hours unless the Story is in a Highlight. A Highlight link is similar but points to a saved collection. If a link has /stories/ in it and it fails, the Story has probably expired, which is the most common reason a Story link breaks.

Short links and tracking parameters

Links shared from the app sometimes use a short domain or add parameters such as ?utm_source=ig_web_copy_link after the main address. These parameters are for tracking and can be removed without breaking the link. A link that ends in /?hl=en is simply a language parameter. When you paste a link into a tool, the tool should ignore these extras and use the shortcode or username, which is what our URL validator on this site does.

Common link mistakes

The most common mistake is copying a link to the Instagram home page or to the Explore page, which contains no post and no shortcode. Another is copying a profile link when you meant a post, which happens when you copy the address while viewing a profile grid. A third is pasting a link with a typo in the shortcode, which points to a different post or to an error. Always open the link in a browser first and check that the content that loads is the one you meant, before you paste it anywhere else.

Shortened links and safety

Instagram links are sometimes shortened when they are shared, especially in messages and bios, and a shortened link hides its destination. That is convenient, but it is also how phishing links are disguised: a short link that promises a free prize or a verified badge usually leads to a fake login page designed to steal your password. The habit that protects you is to preview a link before you tap it: on most phones a long press shows the full address, and on a computer hovering shows it in the corner of the browser. If the address is not instagram.com or a domain you recognise, do not tap it, and never enter your Instagram password on a page you reached through a short link. If a friend sends you a suspicious link, ask them whether they sent it, because hacked accounts often send malicious links to their contacts without the owner knowing.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does an Instagram post URL look like?

instagram.com/p/SHORTCODE/ for a post, instagram.com/reel/SHORTCODE/ for a Reel, and instagram.com/username/ for a profile.

What is the shortcode?

The short code after /p/ or /reel/ that identifies the post. It is unique to that post.

Do Instagram links work without an account?

Public posts usually open in a browser without logging in, but Instagram often asks you to sign in after a few views.
