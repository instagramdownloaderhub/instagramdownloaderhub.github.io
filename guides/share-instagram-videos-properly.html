How to Share Instagram Videos Properly: Links, Reposts, and Credit

Share Instagram videos the right way: links, Story shares, reposts, and giving credit to creators.

Sharing is the point of Instagram, but there is a right way and a wrong way to do it. The right way sends viewers to the creator's original post, keeps the credit intact, and respects the creator's choices. The wrong way copies the video, strips the credit, and re-uploads it as if it were your own. This guide explains the sharing tools Instagram provides and the etiquette that keeps you on good terms with creators.

Share the link

The simplest and best way to share a video is to tap Share and send the link to a friend in a message, or post it in a group chat. The link opens the original post, the creator gets the view, and the credit is automatic. A link is also more reliable than a file, because it always points to the current version of the post, and if the creator updates the caption or fixes an error, the link reflects that. For a video you love, a link is a better gift than a copy.

Share to your Story

Instagram lets you share a post or a Reel to your own Story, with a sticker that links back to the original. This is the sanctioned way to repost, and it is how creators expect their work to be shared. Add your own comment or reaction on top, but do not cover the creator's username or the link sticker. A Story share lasts 24 hours, it drives traffic to the creator, and it is far better than downloading and re-uploading the video yourself.

Reposting and permission

Reposting means downloading a video and uploading it to your own account, and it requires the creator's permission. Many creators are happy to be reposted if you ask and credit them, and many are not, especially if you are a business account that profits from their work. Always ask first, tag the creator in the caption and in the image, and link to the original. If you cannot reach the creator, do not repost. A repost without permission can be reported and removed, and repeat offences can lead to your account being restricted.

Credit and watermarks

When a Reel is downloaded through Instagram's official option, it carries a watermark with the creator's username. Do not crop the watermark out, because it is the credit. If you edit a video you have permission to use, keep the credit visible. Good credit is specific: tag the account, name the creator in the caption, and link to the original post. Creators notice who credits them properly, and they are more likely to collaborate with people who do.

Ask before reposting

Asking permission before you repost is not just polite; it is the fastest way to build a relationship with a creator. A short message saying you loved their video, where you would like to share it, and how you will credit them gets a yes far more often than people expect, because creators like their work to be seen. If the creator says no, respect it without arguing, because the video is theirs to control. If they say yes, keep your promise exactly: tag them, link to the original, and do not edit the video in a way they did not agree to. Some creators will even send you the original file, which looks better than a download. The creators who say yes to you today are the ones who may collaborate with you tomorrow, and a reputation for asking is worth more than any single repost.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I share a video with a friend?

Tap Share and send the link in a message. The link opens the original post and credits the creator.

Can I repost someone's video?

Only with permission, or by using Instagram's Share to Story feature, which keeps the credit intact.

What is the best way to credit a creator?

Tag their account, keep any watermark, and link to the original post rather than re-uploading the file.
