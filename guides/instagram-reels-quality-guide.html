Instagram Reels Quality Guide: Resolution, Size, and Sharp Video

Why Reels look blurry, what resolution they use, and how to keep your downloads and uploads sharp.

Reels are compressed aggressively so they load fast on mobile data, and that compression is why a Reel that looked sharp in your camera roll can look soft on Instagram, and why a downloaded Reel rarely looks as good as the original. Understanding the resolution, the compression, and the settings that affect quality helps you get the best possible result, whether you are watching, downloading, or uploading.

What resolution Reels use

Reels are vertical videos, usually 1080 pixels wide by 1920 tall, which is the vertical version of full HD. Some older Reels are 720 by 1280. Instagram stores the video at this size and then compresses it to keep the file small, which softens fine detail such as hair, text, and foliage. The compression is heavier on mobile data than on Wi-Fi in some cases, because Instagram may serve a lower-quality version when the connection is slow.

Why downloads look softer

A downloaded Reel is the compressed version Instagram stores, not the original file the creator uploaded. That is why a download can look softer than the creator's own copy, and why downloading the same Reel again will not improve it. The watermark Instagram adds to downloaded Reels also slightly affects the image. If you are the creator and you want the sharpest copy, use the original file from your camera roll or your editing app, not the downloaded Reel.

Upload settings that help

If your own Reels look blurry, check three things. First, enable high-quality uploads in Instagram Settings, under Data Usage, where there is an option to upload at the highest quality. Second, upload on Wi-Fi rather than mobile data, because Instagram may reduce quality on a slow connection. Third, start from a sharp original: record in good light, keep the phone steady, and export from your editing app at 1080 by 1920 with a high bitrate. Text in a Reel should be large and bold, because small text is the first thing compression destroys.

Watching in the best quality

If a Reel looks blurry while you watch it, wait a few seconds: Instagram starts playback at a low quality and sharpens it as the connection proves itself. If it stays blurry, check your connection and your data-saver setting, which caps quality to save data. Watching on Wi-Fi with data saver off gives the best result. On a large screen, such as a tablet or a computer, a vertical Reel will always look softer than on a phone, because the same pixels are spread over a bigger display.

Lighting matters more than resolution

A video shot in good light at 720p looks better than a video shot in poor light at 4K, because the camera can capture clean detail only when there is enough light. The cheapest way to improve your Reels is to film near a window during the day, or to use a small ring light in the evening, with the light facing you rather than behind you. Avoid filming with a bright window behind you, because the camera will darken your face to compensate. Keep the lens clean, because a smudged lens softens every video, and hold the phone steady or use a small tripod, because shaky footage looks worse than low resolution. These changes cost little or nothing, and they improve quality more than any setting in the app. Resolution is a number; lighting is what your viewers actually see.

Frequently Asked Questions

What resolution are Instagram Reels?

Reels are usually 1080 by 1921920 pixels, vertical. Instagram compresses them, so they use less data than the original file.

Why does my Reel look blurry after uploading?

Upload on Wi-Fi, enable high-quality uploads in Settings, and start from a sharp original file.

Can I improve a blurry downloaded Reel?

No. The downloaded file only contains the quality Instagram stored, and re-downloading will not add detail.
